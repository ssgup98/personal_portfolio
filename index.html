I joined Restaurant & Bar Design Awards in 2021 in a design research role. Few months in, my role expanded into project coordination, communications, content and partnership strategy, working directly with the Founder.

The Awards partnered with hospitality suppliers, designers and operator groups around the world, offering partners a globally followed platform to build brand visibility and create meaningful business connections.

Partnerships took different forms: paid category sponsorships, service-for-promotion exchanges, and venue partnerships with hospitality groups in exchange for digital visibility.

Challenge

The existing approach to partnership outreach had worked, but the landscape was changing. Partners increasingly wanted flexibility and relevance rather than a fixed list of sponsorship benefits.

The challenge was to move beyond selling predetermined packages and build partnerships around what each organisation actually needed, while balancing sponsorship value, promotional value and internal budget constraints on the partner's side.

Just as importantly, a successful partnership wasn't always one that closed. Maintaining a strong relationship with a potential partner, even when timing or budget made a collaboration impossible, was part of the process.

Approach

I shifted the conversation from “here's what we can offer” to “what are you trying to achieve, and who are you trying to reach?”

Every conversation started with discovery: understanding the partner's objectives, audience and priorities before proposing a solution.

Outreach typically began with a short, personalised email, followed by a call to explore the opportunity in more detail. From there, marketing proposals and campaign strategies were built around the partner's specific goals, rather than adapting those goals to a predetermined package.

The result was a more consultative approach to partnership development: building meaningful connections first, then shaping the right collaboration around them.

Outcome

Over 3.5 years, I helped close 30+ partnerships, with several partners renewing across multiple years.

Partner feedback consistently highlighted two areas of value: direct access to a highly targeted industry audience, and increased visibility and brand recognition through campaigns developed around their specific objectives.

The approach turned partnership development from a transactional sponsorship conversation into an opportunity to understand a business problem, identify a shared objective and build a communications solution around it.

As part of a year-long branding partnership between Restaurant & Bar Design Awards and its 2026 Creative Partner, TYPE was one of the engagement campaigns I managed. It ran across an eight-week window between the shortlist and winners announcements.

Historically, this period saw a drop in social engagement. The objective was twofold: re-engage the Awards' Instagram audience while creating meaningful visibility and value for the Creative Partner.
Challenge

In a digital-only awards format, there is no physical moment for a new Creative Partner to meet or connect with the industry they are now associated with.

For the partner, visibility alone wasn't the goal. They wanted direct access, genuine introductions and opportunities to connect with the designers and operators they would want to work with.

The challenge was to create those one-to-one connections while maintaining audience engagement and driving approximately 600K profile views in 30 days, all within a fixed eight-week window.

Approach

The solution was a simple keyword-led campaign built around a single idea: every space starts as an idea before it becomes physical.

Each shortlisted project was invited to reply with one word that captured the essence of its design.

The Creative Partner then handpicked projects they wanted to connect with, responding personally to each and creating a custom visual around the project's keyword and image. This put the partner directly into conversation with some of the strongest names in hospitality design, turning passive visibility into a genuine introduction.

Participation was deliberately frictionless. Shortlisted projects received one bulk email with a ready-made template, making the response possible in seconds rather than requiring a new piece of content or a lengthy submission.

Behind the campaign, I coordinated the partner workflow through a shared tracker, managing asset status from design through scheduling and publishing and keeping the eight-week timeline moving smoothly.

I also wrote the social media copy for each feature and developed a monthly newsletter around the campaign, which achieved a 42% open rate.

Outcome

62% of shortlisted projects participated, exceeding the platform's 50% engagement benchmark, with 650K+ profile views across 30 days.

The campaign created direct, personal introductions between the Creative Partner and leading hospitality design projects, while giving shortlisted participants additional recognition and value beyond their original entry.

For the platform, it sustained audience engagement through what was historically its quietest period.

Three different goals. One simple mechanism: collaboration.
2025 Awards Ceremony
Project Management Creative Operations Stakeholder Management Cross-functional Coordination Event Design


The 2025 Restaurant & Bar Design Awards ceremony brought together an international network of 400+ hospitality designers and operators, creative partners, sponsors and suppliers for an immersive event in Dubai.

My role was to coordinate the creative production of the ceremony from ideation to execution, across a 12-month timeline. I translated the creative vision into a structured programme of deliverables, timelines and dependencies, coordinating cross-functional teams and keeping multiple workstreams moving towards the same deadline.

From early teaser communications through to the live ceremony, the project spanned event branding, digital campaigns, social content, website assets, video, animation, and on-site digital and wayfinding graphics, working across a broad stakeholder group including the identity partner, creative partners, sound partner, venue and audiovisual teams, social media, event speakers, costume designer, media and more.

BRIEF & CHALLENGES

The brief extended far beyond designing an event.

The ImaginAIre concept for the 2025 Awards needed to live across the entire audience journey, from the first announcement and website experience to social media, newsletters, invitations, ceremony screens, physical branding and post-event content.

The cross-functional ecosystem involved multiple external partners, including Glasfurd & Walker, 4SPACE Design, SPECTRE and Effe Hospitality, alongside the five journey designers, sponsors, venue partners and internal teams.

The challenge was not simply the volume of deliverables. It was coordinating multiple creative disciplines and stakeholders around one idea, while managing different timelines, dependencies, formats and stages of production.

Digital

  • Website landing page and video
  • Instagram and LinkedIn campaigns
  • Newsletter and email marketing assets
  • Event invitations and ticketing assets
  • Teaser and announcement content

Ceremony

  • Presentation templates across 39 categories
  • Event sound and lighting design
  • Narrator slides
  • Brand wall and photocall wall

Post-event

  • Photography and video handovers
  • Press and newsletter assets
  • Social media content
  • Aftermovie communications
PROJECT MANAGEMENT APPROACH

With the project running from September 2024 through the ceremony and into November 2025, my first priority was creating transparency across the entire production lifecycle.

I built and maintained a master timeline covering creative development, stakeholder input, asset handovers, communications milestones and event deadlines, allowing different workstreams to progress in parallel while making their dependencies visible.

I used a combination of Asana, Google Workspace, Later and Figma to manage planning, collaboration, content workflows and creative review.

The project management approach centred around four areas:

01. Planning
Breaking the creative brief into defined workflows, deliverables and milestones.

02. Coordination
Keeping internal teams, creative partners, suppliers and event stakeholders aligned on what was needed, by whom and by when.

03. Dependencies
Identifying where one deliverable or team relied on another, and sequencing the work accordingly.

04. Delivery
Tracking progress through reviews, handovers and final deadlines, ensuring assets were ready for their intended channel or event.

Managing dependencies: A key part of the process was sequencing handovers so that one team's output could become another team's starting point.

For example:

Designer journeys → creative write up and images from Stakeholder A, B, C, D & E → AI videos to be developed by Partner A → sound design by Partner B → final video review by internal team → testing at venue → ceremony presentation

while simultaneously:

Creative concept → identity assets designed by Partner C → sound design by Partner B → social / newsletter content management by internal team → ceremony communications

The project timeline mapped these dependencies alongside the wider communications and event schedule, helping multiple workflows move forward without losing sight of the final event deadline.

This was particularly important as the project moved from concept development into campaign production, and eventually into live-event production, where the number of interdependent deliverables increased significantly.

THE EVENT

By the time the ceremony took place at EPIK Dubai on 27 October 2025, the project had moved through months of creative development, production, reviews and handovers.

My role was to bring those parallel workflows together into a coordinated event delivery, ensuring the visual identity, designer journeys, AI films, sound, presentation system, physical branding and digital communications were all ready to function as part of the same experience.

The result was a live event where the creative concept extended consistently across the screen, the space and the audience journey, supported by the production structure and cross-functional coordination behind it.

Across my work at Restaurant & Bar Design Awards, I've written for very different audiences and purposes: developing partnership proposals, coordinating cross-functional teams, managing sensitive conversations, shaping founder communications and turning ideas into clear, actionable briefs.

My writing changes with the context, but my objective stays the same: make the message clear, make it relevant, and set things in motion.

Partnership & Business Development
{{ writingArrow0 }}
Turning a partner's objectives into a relevant proposition.
CONTEXT
A proposal built around the client's stated objectives: global visibility, thought leadership and meaningful industry connections. Rather than presenting a single sponsorship package, I developed three routes with different levels of engagement and value.
Writing Sample
Hi [Name],

I hope you're well.

Apologies for keeping you waiting.

Following our conversation, we discussed your objectives internally and put together a few options that we believe align well with your goals of increasing global brand visibility and building meaningful connections within the hospitality design industry.

Option 1: Awards Partnership - [Commercial Proposal]
We would love to welcome [Hospitality Brand] as a Partner of the upcoming [Awards Program]. As an Awards Partner, you would receive:
- Global Brand Visibility
Prominent brand placement across the Awards website and newsletters throughout the Awards cycle, reaching a global audience of hospitality and design professionals.
- Thought-Leadership Feature
An interview with your team, creating a dedicated feature around [relevant design category/topic], promoted across our editorial and social channels.
- Personal Introductions
A senior member of our team would personally introduce you to a curated selection of influential hospitality professionals and decision-makers. The list would be developed collaboratively with your team.
- Exclusive Branding Rights
Permission to use the [Awards Programme] partnership identity across your website and marketing materials.

Option 2: Brand Spotlight - [Commercial Proposal]
This option provides year-round visibility through:
- Global Brand Visibility
Prominent brand placement across the Awards website and newsletters throughout the Awards cycle.
- Thought-Leadership Feature
An interview with your team, creating a dedicated feature around [relevant design category/topic], promoted across our editorial and social channels.

Option 3: Exclusive Thought-Leadership Feature - [Commercial Proposal]
We discussed the proposed [specific category] internally and, unfortunately, don't believe it would be the strongest fit for our audience. However, we do see a strong opportunity to position [Hospitality Brand] as a thought leader within [relevant category/topic].

We would interview your team and create a dedicated feature exploring trends, innovation and the future of the category, promoted across our newsletter and social channels.

This would allow your team to share its expertise with a global audience while gaining exposure among designers, operators and hospitality professionals.

Please let us know your thoughts on the above options. We'd be happy to discuss them further and explore how we can tailor a collaboration to best support your objectives.

Best,
Simran Gupte
Managing Creative Differences
{{ writingArrow2 }}
Resolving creative differences between cross-functional teams to avoid conflict.
CONTEXT
When two creative teams were developing different parts of the same identity, the communication needed to protect both sides' work while finding a way to create cohesion. The message acknowledged both perspectives, proposed a practical route forward and created a space for the teams to resolve it together.
Writing Sample
Hi [Name],

Just to share an update - we met with [Brand Name] yesterday, where they presented their concept for the trophy box design. Here's the presentation for your reference: [Link]

Following the discussion, and to make sure neither of your creative efforts or time is wasted, we talked through a possible way to bring everything together into one cohesive identity i.e. there needs to be a very strong visual connection.

The idea would be for [Brand Name] to share the colour codes for the identity colour palette so [Brand Name] can align with this. Also, [Brand Name] could, in very small doses, begin incorporating some of the patterns [Brand Name] has been developing, without changing the overall theme. One example discussed was integrating these patterns into the non-header webpage videos within your existing video collage.

We definitely don’t want to disrupt your flow of work leading up to the identity reveal, so any of the above can be introduced later and at your own pace.

I think it would be helpful to have a quick meeting with everyone for more clarity, and it would also be great for both teams to meet directly. How about [Day] [Date] at [Time]?

If that works, I’ll share a calendar invite.

Best,
Simran Gupte
